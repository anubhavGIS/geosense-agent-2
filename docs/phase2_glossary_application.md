# Phase 2 Glossary Application (Part G) — eight terms as they were used in this project

**1. Sentinel-2 / Band.** Every image in Phase 2 is a Sentinel-2 surface-reflectance composite from Google Earth Engine (`COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED`): the median of all scenes of a year with less than 10 % cloud (28 scenes for 2018, 55 for 2023) over the 12.6 × 24.5 km Kolkata study area at 10 m. Six bands were kept — B2 blue, B3 green, B4 red, B8 near-infrared, B11 and B12 short-wave infrared — because they carry the spectral information that separates water, vegetation and built-up land, and because they are the six bands the Prithvi foundation model was pre-trained on. The QA60 band was requested only for its cloud flags.

**2. Normalisation.** The composites arrive as 16-bit integers (reflectance × 10,000). `preprocess_imagery.py` masks clouds (all bands > 3000) and the −32768 nodata edge, then divides by 10,000 and clips to 0–1, so every network input lies in a small range. The Prithvi wrapper additionally subtracts the per-band means and divides by the standard deviations published with the model, because a pre-trained network expects the input distribution it was trained on.

**3. NDVI and NDWI.** NDVI = (B8 − B4)/(B8 + B4) and NDWI = (B3 − B8)/(B3 + B8) were computed for both years as full rasters (`ndvi_2023.npy` mean 0.284, `ndvi_2018.npy` mean 0.281; NDWI −0.294 / −0.299). They did three jobs: they made the training labels (water where NDWI > 0.3, dense vegetation where NDVI > 0.4, and so on), they are returned by `classify_imagery()` for any location, and their difference between 2018 and 2023 is the change signal.

**4. Chip.** The 2448 × 1257-pixel composites are too large for a network, so `chip_images.py` cut each into 224 × 224 × 6 tiles with a 174-pixel stride (50-pixel overlap): 13 × 6 = 78 chips per year, 156 in all, each 1.2 MB of float32. The chips are the training examples; the label patch of a chip is read from the year's label raster at the same row/column offset, which the chip's filename records.

**5. U-Net / Skip connection.** `train_unet.py` builds `smp.Unet` with a ResNet-34 encoder (24,446,357 parameters, six input channels, five classes). The encoder halves the resolution five times to learn what is where; the decoder doubles it back; the skip connections copy each encoder resolution straight across to the decoder, which is why the U-Net's maps keep 10 m detail and why it reached 87.17 % accuracy and a mean IoU of 66.63 % on the 32 held-out chips. Augmentation (flips, 90° rotations, brightness jitter) multiplied 124 training chips into effectively a thousand views and kept the model from memorising them (overfitting).

**6. Foundation model / Prithvi-100M / Fine-tuning.** `finetune_prithvi.py` downloads NASA–IBM's Prithvi-EO-1.0-100M from HuggingFace (a ViT-B/16 masked autoencoder pre-trained on Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2 imagery), loads 149 of its 254 checkpoint tensors into a one-date encoder, freezes the patch embedding and transformer blocks 0–9, and trains only blocks 10–11, the final normalisation and a new two-path segmentation head — 16,906,469 of 88,966,373 parameters (19.0 %) — with AdamW at 1e-5 for the backbone and 1e-4 for the head. Fine-tuning took 140 s for 40 epochs on the laptop GPU and reached 76.17 % validation accuracy.

**7. IoU (Intersection over Union).** `evaluate_models.py` scores both models on the same 32 held-out chips: for each class, the pixels both the prediction and the label call that class, divided by the pixels either calls it. Mean IoU exposes what pixel accuracy hides — Bare Land (2.7 % of pixels) scores 39.1 % IoU for the U-Net but 3.8 % for the fine-tuned Prithvi, which absorbs it into Urban — and it is the number on which the U-Net was chosen as the model behind `image_classifier.py`.

**8. Change detection.** `classify_imagery()` cuts the same 224 × 224 window from the 2018 and 2023 composites, computes NDVI for each, and flags `change_flag = True` when the mean NDVI inside the requested radius moved by more than 0.15, reporting the direction (vegetation loss or gain) and the share of pixels that changed; `find_change_hotspots()` ranks every 500 m block of the study area by the same difference to point the user to where change is largest — in 2018→2023 the strongest gain (+0.27) lies in the East Kolkata Wetlands and the strongest loss (−0.17) on the Howrah side of the Hooghly. The baseline year is 2018 because the harmonised Sentinel-2 collection begins in 2017.
